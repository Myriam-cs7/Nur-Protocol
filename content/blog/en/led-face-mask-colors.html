Red, blue, yellow, green, cyan, white, magenta… A seven-colour LED mask promises a light for every need. But these colours do not all rest on the same level of evidence, and it is how you use them that makes the difference. Here is what each one does, what the studies say, and how to combine them in a skincare routine.

Why an LED mask has several colours

Each colour corresponds to a wavelength, measured in nanometres. The longer it is, the deeper the light reaches into the skin, so blue works at the surface while red goes further down. That is why the colours are not interchangeable, and why a mask is not better simply because it shows more of them. What counts is using the right light, at the right time, consistently.

On NŪR Protocol, the seven settings range from 440 to 633 nanometres, and each one is listed on the product page.

Red, the most studied colour

Red, at 633 nm on NŪR Protocol, is the reference light for firmness, texture and fine lines, and also the best documented. A randomised, double-blind trial published in 2007 compared red light at 633 nm with a sham light, twice a week for four weeks, and reported improvements in wrinkles and elasticity on the treated side. It is the colour found in most anti-ageing routines.

Blue, for blemish-prone skin

Blue, between 440 and 450 nm on NŪR Protocol, works at the surface. It has been studied for more than twenty years for acne-prone skin, especially in combination with red. A 2025 meta-analysis in JAMA Dermatology on at-home LED devices concluded that blue and red light are effective on mild to moderate acne, more so when combined. Blue does call for real care with the eyes, which is why the eye protectors supplied are always worn.

Magenta, red and blue together

Magenta is not a colour in its own right but the combination of red and blue in the same session. It happens to be the best studied combination for blemishes. As early as 2000, a study in the British Journal of Dermatology of about a hundred people with mild to moderate acne found better results with blue and red together than with blue alone.

Yellow, for radiance and tone

Yellow, between 550 and 630 nm on NŪR Protocol, is associated with radiance, redness and an uneven tone. A few studies have looked at it, including a 2005 study of 90 people that reported smoother skin and a more even tone, though without a control group and with authors linked to the device tested. Yellow is therefore a promising colour, best understood as a complement in a routine rather than a proven treatment.

Green, the colour for dark spots

Green, between 540 and 550 nm, is promoted for dark spots. Research on it is still very recent, with a first study published in 2025 observing less melanin on one half of the face after eight weeks of a close green light. That is encouraging, but one study is not enough, and green is used today as an option within an anti-spot routine, alongside sun protection, which remains essential.

Cyan and white, the supporting lights

Cyan, between 440 and 550 nm, is usually offered for reactive skin, and white, covering 440 to 630 nm, blends several wavelengths into a broad light used for skin renewal. They are the two colours with the least specific research. They have their place in a varied routine, without any proven effect of their own.

How to combine the colours in a routine

The real question is not which colour is best, but which one to use and when. Skin that shows the years does not need the same light as blemish-prone skin, and needs change from week to week. Using a colour at random, or always the same one out of habit, means missing what makes a seven-colour mask worthwhile.

FAQ

Which LED mask colour is best for wrinkles?

Red is the most studied light for firmness, texture and fine lines. In a NŪR Protocol routine, the NŪR Skincoach assessment tells you when to use it and how often.

Which LED mask colour is best for acne?

Blue, ideally combined with red, which is magenta. It is the best documented combination for mild to moderate acne. Severe acne is a matter for a dermatologist.

Which LED mask colour is best for dark spots?

Green and yellow are offered for dark spots and uneven tone, but studies are still few. Daily sun protection remains essential.

Can I use several colours in the same week?

Yes, that is the point of a seven-colour mask. Your personalised routine spreads the colours according to your skin’s needs, without going beyond three to five sessions a week.

What do the colours of an LED mask mean?

Each colour is a wavelength that reaches more or less deeply into the skin. Red is associated with firmness, blue with blemishes, yellow with radiance, green with dark spots, cyan with reactive skin and white with renewal.

Which LED face mask is the best? Every brand answers that it is its own, and so would we. Rather than a ranking that could not be neutral, here are the criteria that really tell two masks apart, the questions to ask before buying, and the claims that should make you wary. They apply to every mask, ours included.

Why we do not publish a ranking

We make an LED mask, so a list of “the ten best masks” written by us would be advertising dressed up as advice. A ranking also says little about your own skin. A mask that suits blemish-prone skin is not necessarily the one that suits skin that is losing firmness, and the price tag is a poor guide to quality. What you can compare, on the other hand, are facts that any serious brand is able to give you.

Wavelengths given in nanometres

A colour is not a specification. “Red light” can mean very different wavelengths, and it is the wavelength, measured in nanometres, that determines how deep the light reaches into the skin. The studies on firmness and fine lines mostly use red between about 630 and 660 nm, and those on blemishes use blue around 415 to 450 nm. A brand that only gives colour names, without any figures, leaves you unable to check anything.

Nor is a mask better simply because it shows more colours. Seven settings are only useful if you know which one to use and when, which is what our article on LED face mask colours explains. On NŪR Protocol, each wavelength is listed on the product page, with red at 633 nm.

Irradiance, the figure that is often missing

Irradiance is the power of the light that reaches the skin, expressed in milliwatts per square centimetre. Together with session length, it determines the dose of light received. It is the hardest figure to find on a product page, and yet it is the one that tells a real device apart from a decorative one. There is no single ideal value, since studies use very different doses, but a brand should be able to give you its own. NŪR Protocol announces 30 mW/cm².

The number of LEDs and what they cover

The number of LEDs gives an idea of how evenly the light is spread, provided you know what it covers. Some masks only treat the face, others leave out the area around the mouth or the jaw. Yet the neck gives away age as much as the face, sometimes more, and it is the same skin, simply thinner. Treating one without the other is a little like putting make-up on one eye only, and nobody goes out like that.

That is why NŪR Protocol is sold as a whole, with 104 LEDs, 60 on the face mask and 44 on the neck panel, which can be used at the same time. We explain this choice in our article on the LED face and neck mask.

Comfort and fit

A mask you do not enjoy wearing ends up in a drawer, and an LED mask only works with regular use. Flexible silicone follows the shape of the face and keeps the LEDs close to the skin, whereas a rigid shell suits some faces better than others. Weight matters, and so does not being tied to a socket during the session. NŪR Protocol is made of flexible silicone, and its controller runs on a rechargeable battery, for sessions of about ten minutes.

Eye protection and the user manual

Intense blue light can be harmful to the retina, so a serious mask comes with eye protection and a manual that states who should not use it. Look for a list of contraindications, such as pregnancy, light sensitivity or photosensitising medication, and for a sensitivity test before the first session. A brand that claims its mask suits everyone, without any reservation, is not doing you a favour. All of this is set out in our article on whether LED face masks are safe.

Knowing which light to use, and when

This criterion rarely appears in comparisons, and yet it decides the result. A seven-colour mask used at random, or always on the same setting out of habit, gives far less than a mask used with a clear routine. Some brands provide a simple leaflet, others a set programme that is the same for everyone.

NŪR Protocol comes with NŪR Skincoach, an app that assesses your skin with a short questionnaire, and a selfie if you choose, and that assessment gives your personalised routine, from the colours to their order and frequency. The user manual recommends three to five sessions a week, in four-week cycles.

Claims to be wary of

A precise percentage of wrinkle reduction, “visible results in seven days” or a mask said to be “clinically proven” without any published study to back it up should all make you cautious. The research on LED light is real, but the trials are often small, and the results build up over several weeks. Before and after photos are useful only if the brand says how they were taken, under the same light, from the same angle and after how long. Price, warranty and returns

A good mask is meant to be used for years, so the warranty and the return conditions count as much as the price. Check how long the warranty lasts, whether you can return the mask if it does not suit you, and whether the battery and the controller are covered. FAQ

What is the best LED face mask?

There is no single best mask for everyone. The best one for you publishes its wavelengths and irradiance, covers the areas you want to treat, is comfortable enough to wear several times a week, and comes with clear guidance on how to use it.

Is a mask with more colours better?

Not in itself. Red and blue are the most studied lights. Additional colours are useful only if you know when to use them, which is the role of a personalised routine.

Is a silicone LED mask better than a rigid one?

Flexible silicone follows the shape of the face more closely and is often more comfortable, which helps with regular use. A rigid mask can suit some faces well. Comfort matters, since the results depend on consistency.

What irradiance should an LED mask have?

There is no single ideal value, as studies use very different doses. What matters is that the brand publishes its figure and a clear session length. NŪR Protocol announces 30 mW/cm², for sessions of about ten minutes.

Is there an LED mask for the face and the neck?

Yes. NŪR Protocol combines a face mask and a neck panel, with 60 and 44 LEDs, that plug into the same controller and can be used at the same time.

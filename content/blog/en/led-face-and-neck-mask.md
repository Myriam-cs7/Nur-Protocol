# LED face and neck mask: why the neck needs its own panel

*Published by NŪR Protocol. Last reviewed: [publication date]. NŪR Protocol sells a silicone LED face and neck mask; this article explains how ours is built and used, and says where the evidence stops.*

Most LED masks stop at the jawline. The neck and the décolleté, which see as much sun and as many years as the face, are left out of the session. A face and neck LED mask adds a second panel for those areas. This page explains what that panel covers, how it is fitted, and how it fits into a skincare routine.

## What a face-only mask leaves out

A face mask is shaped around the eyes, nose and mouth. Its lower edge follows the jaw. Below it, the front of the neck and the décolleté receive no light at all, whatever the programme.

That matters for one simple reason: the neck is often the first area people notice and the last one their skincare routine reaches. Creams applied to the face often stop at the jaw too. Dermatology texts describe neck skin as thinner than facial skin, with fewer oil glands, which is one reason it is usually treated gently.

## Can you use a regular LED face mask on your neck?

Not in a useful way. A rigid face mask cannot sit flat on the neck, and a flexible one is cut for the features of the face: the light would land unevenly, and the eye openings would sit where there is nothing to protect. If the neck matters to you, it needs a panel cut for it.

## One session for face and neck

Because both pieces run from one controller, the face and the neck are treated in the same session, not one after the other. In practice:

1. **Cleanse and dry** the face and the neck. No make-up, and none of the products your routine excludes before a session.
2. **Fit the eye protectors**, then the face mask and its strap.
3. **Fit the neck panel**: the band around the front of the neck, the lower plate on the décolleté, the strap closed at the back without pulling.
4. **Wipe off any moisture**, connect both pieces to the controller, and switch on.
5. **Choose the light and the brightness** set by your routine. The default timer is 10 minutes; 5, 15 and 20 minutes are also available.
6. **Remove both pieces** when the timer ends, and continue with your skincare.

The controller switches off by itself when the timer reaches zero.

## What the research says about the neck

Honestly: very little. Clinical studies of LED light on skin ageing have mostly looked at the face, and the few that include the neck are small. The benefits documented for red and blue light (see [LED face mask benefits](/blogs/news/led-face-mask-benefits)) were measured on facial skin. We do not claim more for the neck than the research supports.

What a neck panel does guarantee is simpler: if you want the neck and décolleté in your LED routine, the light actually reaches them.

## What to watch on the neck

To judge your own results, treat the neck like the face:

- take a starting photo of the neck and décolleté, in the same window and at the same time of day, head in the same position;
- repeat it every two weeks in the same conditions, and compare the photos side by side;
- note new products, sun exposure and anything else that changed.

Head position changes the look of the neck more than anything else, so keep it identical from one photo to the next.

## Cleaning and storage

After each session, wipe both silicone pieces with a soft towel or a wet wipe, let them air-dry in a well-ventilated room, then store them in the dust-proof bag. Avoid direct contact with water. Do not use the device while it is charging, and charge it fully before the first use.

## Who should not use it

The NŪR Protocol user manual lists the people who should not use the device: pregnant or breastfeeding people; people with light sensitivity or taking photosensitising medication; people with a hereditary eye condition or a skin disease such as lupus, photosensitive eczema or albinism. Before the first session, the manual asks for a 10-minute test on the forearm: if redness or itching appears, stop and see a dermatologist. NŪR Protocol is intended for adults aged 18 and over.

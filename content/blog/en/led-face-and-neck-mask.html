Most LED masks stop at the jawline. The neck and the décolleté, which see as much sun and as many years as the face, are simply left out of the session. A face and neck mask gives them a panel of their own. Here is why it matters, how it is fitted, and how it fits into your skincare routine.

The part of the routine everyone forgets

A face mask is drawn around the eyes, the nose and the mouth, and its lower edge follows the jaw. Below it, the neck and the décolleté receive no light at all, whatever programme you choose.

Yet the neck gives away the passing years as much as the face, sometimes more. It is often the first place people notice and the last place a skincare routine reaches, since creams too tend to stop at the jaw. Dermatology texts describe the skin of the neck as thinner than that of the face, with fewer oil glands, which is why it deserves at least as much care.

Why a face mask cannot do the job

A rigid mask cannot rest flat against the neck, and a flexible one is cut for the features of the face. The light would land unevenly, and the eye openings would sit where there is nothing to protect. If the neck matters to you, it needs a panel designed for it.

One session for face and neck

Start with a clean, dry face and neck, without make-up or any product your routine leaves out before a session. Put the eye protectors in place, then the face mask and its strap. Fit the neck panel next, with the band around the front of the neck and the lower part on the décolleté, and close its strap at the back without pulling. Wipe away any moisture, connect both pieces and switch on.

Choose the light and the brightness set by your routine. The timer runs for ten minutes by default, and can also be set to five, fifteen or twenty. When it reaches zero the controller switches off by itself, and all that is left is to remove both pieces and carry on with your skincare.

One routine for both

Since the session is shared, the face and the neck receive the same light for the same time. What differs is what you watch and which skincare you pair. NŪR Skincoach, the app that comes with NŪR Protocol, assesses your skin with a short questionnaire, and a selfie if you choose, and that assessment gives your personalised routine. The neck is part of the assessment from the start, never an afterthought. The user manual recommends three to five sessions a week, in four-week cycles, and using the mask more often will not bring faster results.

What research says about the neck

Very little, to be honest. Clinical studies of LED light and skin ageing have mostly looked at the face, and the few that include the neck are small. The benefits documented for red and blue light, described in our article on LED face mask benefits, were measured on facial skin, and we claim nothing more for the neck than the research supports. What a neck panel does guarantee is simpler. If you want your neck and décolleté in your LED routine, the light truly reaches them.

Following your progress

Treat the neck as you treat the face. Before the first session, photograph your neck and décolleté by the same window, at the same time of day and with your head in exactly the same position, then repeat every two weeks and compare the pictures side by side. Head position changes the look of the neck more than anything else, so keep it identical, and note any new product or change in sun exposure along the way.

Care and storage

After each session, wipe both silicone pieces with a soft towel or a damp wipe, let them air-dry in a well-ventilated room, and store them in their dust-proof bag. Keep them away from direct contact with water. The device is not used while it charges, and it should be fully charged before its first use.

Who should not use it

The user manual rules out NŪR Protocol during pregnancy and breastfeeding, for people with light-sensitive skin or taking photosensitising medication, and for people with a hereditary eye condition or a skin disease such as lupus, photosensitive eczema or albinism. Before the first session, it asks for a ten-minute test on the forearm, and any redness or itching means stopping and seeing a dermatologist. NŪR Protocol is intended for adults aged 18 and over.
